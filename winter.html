Choose your winter experience

Most winter trips lean one of three ways. Start with the one that sounds most like the break you want.

  • Mountain escapes

    Trade busy streets for snowy valleys, viewpoints and quiet village walks. Some people come to ski or snowshoe; others simply come for the views and the calm.

  • City breaks

    Fill short days with museums, seasonal markets, concerts and long lunches. So much happens indoors that a city break copes well with cold or wet weather.

  • Cozy stays

    Sometimes the place you stay is the trip: a chalet with a fireplace, a lodge with a view, an evening with nowhere to be. Choose a warm base and let slow days be part of the plan.

Featured winter destinations

Four countries to start your research. Each covers very different regions, so winter conditions vary by place, altitude and year.

Switzerland

Alpine villages linked by scenic railways

Panoramic rail routes cross the Swiss Alps past valleys, lakes and mountain villages such as Zermatt and car-free Wengen. Trains, buses and mountain railways let you pair a city stay with a few days in the mountains.

Best for: Mountain scenery

  • Austria

    Advent markets and coffee-house culture

    Vienna and Salzburg hold Advent markets in the weeks before Christmas, Salzburg’s in the squares of its Baroque old town. Between visits, Vienna’s traditional coffee houses are a warm place to slow down.

    Best for: City exploration

  • Finland

    Snowy forests and the polar night

    Much of Lapland enters the polar night, or kaamos, in December and January, and in the far north the sun does not rise for weeks. Reindeer and husky rides are classic ways to explore its snowy forests.

    Best for: Arctic nature

  • Canada

    Historic streets and Rocky Mountain trails

    Winter looks very different across such a large country. Two well-known choices are Old Québec, a UNESCO World Heritage site, and Banff National Park in Alberta, with winter trails for snowshoeing and cross-country skiing.

    Best for: Cities and national parks

Before you plan

In the Northern Hemisphere, winter usually means December to February, but snow, daylight and opening times vary by region, altitude and year.

  • Check the weather and daylight

    Mountain weather can change quickly, and days get much shorter the further north you go. Look at a local forecast and sunrise and sunset times shortly before you travel.

  • Confirm opening times and transport

    Cable cars, mountain roads, attractions and some bus or rail routes may follow seasonal timetables or close in bad weather. Check with each operator before you set off.

  • Choose a city or mountain base

    A city suits museums, markets and easy connections; a mountain village suits snow and quiet views. Deciding on your base first makes stays easier to compare.
